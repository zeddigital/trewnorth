Should Your Small Business Register for GST?
Understanding when and why your small business should register for GST (Goods and Services Tax) is vital for compliance and financial planning. In Australia, GST is a 10% tax on most goods and services sold or consumed domestically. To understand how gst work, businesses collect GST on their sales and then remit it to the Australian Taxation Office (ATO). This guide will clearly explain everything you need to know about registering your small business for GST, and help you determine if it’s the right move for your business.

What Exactly is GST and How Does it Work?
GST is a broad-based tax that impacts nearly every business transaction in Australia. Businesses registered for GST must collect an additional 10% on taxable supplies, these are goods and services that are subject to GST under Australian law, excluding GST-free or input-taxed items, which they later remit to the Australian Taxation Office (ATO).

The total price charged to customers often includes GST, meaning the GST portion is embedded within the sale amount. When a price is marked as ‘gst included’, it means the GST is already part of the total amount paid by the customer, and you can calculate the GST portion by dividing the total by 11.

Key GST Points for Small Businesses:
You are responsible for charging GST on your taxable sales once your business is registered.

You pay GST on business-related expenses but can claim credits for GST you’ve paid.

You report GST through a Business Activity Statement (BAS), which is a summary report submitted to the ATO detailing GST collected from sales and GST paid on expenses.

Calculating Your GST Turnover – Are You Over the Threshold?
Understanding your goods and services tax (GST) turnover is essential for every small business owner. The GST turnover threshold is $75,000 for most businesses and $150,000 for non profit organisations. To work out if you need to register for GST, add up your total sales from all your business activities, but exclude any GST-free or input-taxed sales. This means you only count the value of goods and services that are subject to the services tax GST.

Regularly monitoring your GST turnover helps you stay on top of your GST obligations and avoid unexpected tax issues. If you’re unsure how to calculate your GST turnover or whether you need to register for GST, a registered tax agent can help you review your figures and ensure you remain compliant. Staying proactive with your GST turnover calculations is a smart move for any small business or non profit organisation.

When Must You Register for GST?
The most critical factor determining GST registration is your annual business turnover, specifically whether you exceed the $75,000 annual turnover threshold, also known as the GST threshold, for most businesses.

The ATO introduced this $75,000 GST threshold and a simplified BAS format in July 2017 to make GST reporting and compliance easier for small businesses.

Not every business is required to register for GST; only those whose turnover meets or exceeds the threshold, or who meet other specific criteria, must do so.

Mandatory GST Registration:
Your annual turnover reaches or exceeds $75,000.

You expect your annual turnover to reach or exceed $75,000 in the current financial year.

You provide taxi or rideshare services (GST registration is compulsory regardless of turnover).

Your business structure, such as operating as a sole trader, can influence your GST registration requirements. For example, sole traders must register for GST if their turnover exceeds the threshold.

Voluntary GST Registration:
You are considering voluntarily registering for GST even though your turnover is below the $75,000 threshold.

Some businesses choose this option to claim more GST credits or refunds on their business expenses.

How GST Impacts Your Cash Flow
Being registered for GST can have a big impact on your business’s cash flow. When you charge GST on your sales, you’re collecting money on behalf of the ATO, which means you’ll need to pay GST to the government regularly. On the flip side, you can claim GST credits on your business purchases, which helps reduce the amount you owe.

For small businesses and sole traders, managing cash flow is crucial. If you don’t keep track of your GST obligations, you might find yourself short when it’s time to pay the ATO. Using accounting software or working with a bookkeeper can help you monitor your GST payments and credits, making it easier to claim GST credits and avoid cash flow surprises. Staying organised ensures you meet your GST obligations and keep your business running smoothly.

How to Calculate GST on Your Sales and Purchases
Calculating GST is a straightforward process once you know the basics. For your sales, simply multiply the GST-exclusive price by 1.1 to get the GST-inclusive price. For example, if you sell a service for $100 (excluding GST), the total price including GST will be $110.

When it comes to business purchases, you can claim GST credits by working out the GST component. To do this, divide the GST-inclusive price by 11 to find the GST amount, or divide the total by 1.1 to get the GST-exclusive price, then multiply by the GST rate (10%). Using a GST calculator or accounting software can make it even easier to calculate GST and claim GST credits accurately. This helps you stay on top of your GST obligations and ensures you claim all the GST you’re entitled to.

How to Register Your Small Business for GST
Registering For GST
GST registration can be straightforward if you follow the proper steps:

If you are a new business, you should consider your GST and other business registrations early to ensure compliance from the start.

Easy Steps to Register:
Obtain an Australian Business Number (ABN).

Complete the GST registration process through the ATO’s Business Portal or via an accountant.

Set up accounting systems to handle GST invoicing and reporting.

Ensure you issue a tax invoice for all taxable sales, as this official document is required for GST compliance and allows customers to claim GST credits.

GST and Invoicing Things You Need to Know
If your business is registered for GST, it’s important to get your invoicing right. Every invoice you issue should clearly show the GST amount, either as a separate line or included in the total price. Make sure your invoices include all the required details: a description of the goods or services sold, the date of the sale, and the GST amount.

A well-organised invoicing system helps your small business stay compliant with GST obligations and makes it easier to track the GST you’ve charged and collected. Using invoicing software or working with a bookkeeper can help you create professional, compliant invoices and avoid any issues with the ATO. Proper invoicing ensures you include GST correctly and keeps your business running smoothly.

Staying GST Compliant – What You Need to Know
Once registered, staying compliant is crucial to avoid fines and penalties.

GST reporting is done through BAS statements, which must be accurate and submitted on time to the ATO.

For GST reporting, businesses can choose between the accruals basis and cash basis. On an accruals basis, GST is reported when invoices are issued, while on a cash basis, GST is reported only when payment is received.

Staying compliant with GST and other taxes is essential for business success.

Essential GST Compliance Tips:
Keep accurate and timely records of all transactions.

Track GST on all items sold to ensure accurate reporting and compliance.

Regularly submit your BAS to the ATO.

Clearly indicate GST amounts on invoices to customers.

Consult regularly with your accountant to ensure ongoing compliance.

What Happens If You Don’t Register? Risks and Penalties
Failing to register for GST when you’re required to can lead to serious consequences for your business. The ATO may impose penalties and fines, and you’ll miss out on claiming GST credits for your business purchases, which can hurt your bottom line. Not registering for GST can also damage your business’s reputation and credibility, especially if you’re found to be non-compliant.

For sole traders and small businesses, it’s essential to take GST registration seriously and meet all your GST obligations. If you’re unsure about registering for GST or need help with the process, a registered tax agent or accountant can guide you through GST registration and help you stay compliant. Prioritising GST compliance protects your business from unnecessary tax risks and keeps you on the right side of the law.
